SPIRIT AND OPPORTUNITY

In 2003, two rovers began a journey to the Red Planet.

Their names were Spirit and Opportunity.

Their mission was to investigate the Martian surface and search for clues about its ancient past.

The rovers revealed that Mars was once rich in water, with conditions that may have supported microbial life.

Meet the explorers

A shared mission. Two different worlds.

NASA launched Spirit and Opportunity in 2003 to investigate whether places on Mars had once been wet and could have had conditions suitable for life. They were robotic geologists, sent to explore different landing sites.

Spirit · Gusev Crater

Spirit

Spirit landed on January 4, 2004. It explored Gusev Crater and the Columbia Hills, finding rocks and soil that recorded a complex history involving water and volcanic processes.

Opportunity · Meridiani Planum

Opportunity

Opportunity landed on January 25, 2004. It found evidence that liquid water had affected rocks in its region and travelled much farther than its planned mission.

Opportunity explores the craters

Opportunity investigates Endurance and Victoria craters, examining rock layers that help scientists reconstruct Mars's geological history.

Spirit's final communication

After becoming stuck in soft soil, Spirit stops communicating. NASA's final communication attempt takes place on March 22, 2010.

Opportunity keeps travelling

Opportunity reaches Endeavour Crater and continues its science work. A planet-wide dust storm in 2018 blocks sunlight from its solar panels.

The twin mission concludes

NASA declares the Opportunity mission complete after months without contact. The two rovers had far exceeded their planned lifetimes.

Science behind the story

What did the twins discover?

Spirit's scientific clues

Spirit found silica-rich soil and rocks altered by water in the Columbia Hills. These clues helped scientists investigate ancient environments involving hot water.

Opportunity's scientific clues

Opportunity found iron-rich spherules and layered rocks showing that liquid water had affected the region in the distant past. Its crater explorations exposed layers from Mars's geological history.

The rovers did not discover life. They found evidence that helped scientists understand whether ancient Mars had environments where liquid water could exist.
